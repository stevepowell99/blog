Measuring What Matters

FRDS — the Financial Reporting and Data System — captures the annual performance data that shows how National Societies serve their communities and how that service is changing over time.

01 · The Network

A presence in every corner of the world

The Red Cross Red Crescent Movement is the world's largest humanitarian network. 191 National Societies operate in 191 countries and territories — meaning there is almost no place on earth without a local Red Cross or Red Crescent.

FRDS connects all 191, aggregating annual data on their people, finances, and programmes into a single comparable dataset for the first time in the Movement's history.

In 2024, 176 National Societies submitted complete FRDS returns — a record reporting rate of 92%.

02 · Volunteers

17.5 million people giving their time

Volunteers are the Movement's defining strength. In 2024, 17.5 million volunteers gave their time and skills — more than any other humanitarian organisation in the world.

The Asia Pacific region is home to the largest share, with National Societies in India, Bangladesh, and the Philippines among the largest by volunteer numbers.

Overall volunteer numbers have grown +12% since 2020, reversing the decline seen during the COVID-19 pandemic years.

03 · People Reached

1.9 billion people reached in a single year

Across all programmes, National Societies collectively reached 1.9 billion people in 2024. Disaster relief and emergency health remain the largest programme areas by numbers served.

Health and livelihoods programmes are growing fastest, reflecting a strategic shift towards longer-term resilience alongside emergency response.

MENA National Societies saw the sharpest increase — a +34% rise in people reached — driven by responses in Gaza and Sudan.

04 · Emergency Operations

A rising tide of climate-driven crises

National Societies launched 156 major emergency operations in 2024. Climate-related events — floods, droughts, extreme heat — now account for 67% of all operations, up from 52% in 2020.

The average operation now lasts 8.3 months, compared to 5.1 months in 2019 — a sign that crises are becoming more protracted and complex.

Operations linked to conflict and displacement remain at a structurally elevated level, with no sign of declining across the five-year period.
