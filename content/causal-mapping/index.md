Causal mapping is my main line of work. You take what people say about what causes what, code those statements as causal links, and assemble them into a map you can search and quote back to source.

I co-founded [Causal Map Ltd](https://causalmap.app) in 2019 to build software for this, and I now spend most of my time on it.

## The method

- [Causal mapping for evaluators](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13563890231196601), with James Copestake and Fiona Remnant, in *Evaluation* (2024). The paper that sets out the approach, and there is a [short summary](https://garden.causalmap.app/summary-eval2024) if you want the argument without the journal.
- [Minimalist coding for causal mapping](https://garden.causalmap.app/minimalist), why we code a link as nothing more than one cause, one effect and a quote.
- [Causal mapping in evaluation](https://garden.causalmap.app/evaluation-intro), including how it complements contribution analysis and outcome harvesting, and what poor data quality does to it.
- [Causal mapping as qualitative data analysis](https://garden.causalmap.app/qda-intro), for readers coming from QDA rather than from evaluation.

## Book chapters

- [Theories of change and causal maps in Ghana](https://garden.causalmap.app/summary-ghana), with James Copestake and Fiona Remnant: what happens when you set a programme's official theory of change against a map coded from what people said.
- [Qualitative causal mapping in evaluations](https://garden.causalmap.app/summary-health), on health services, with Fiona Remnant, James Copestake and Melanie Channon.

All of this and more is in the [Causal Map Garden](https://garden.causalmap.app), where we write up the method as we go: the [article summaries](https://garden.causalmap.app/articles-intro), the [working papers](https://garden.causalmap.app/working-papers) and the [bibliography](https://garden.causalmap.app/bibliography).
