A theory of change sets out how a programme is supposed to work: what it does, what it expects to follow, and why. Most of my evaluation work starts there, and a lot of my writing on causal mapping grew out of dissatisfaction with how theories of change are usually drawn.

[Theorymaker](https://theorymaker.netlify.app) is my free web app for writing one as text and getting a diagram back. Various versions of it have been up since about 2012. I shut it down at the start of 2024 and rebuilt it, with AI help, at the end of 2025.

I also wrote a set of <a href="/static/slides.html" data-router-ignore>slides</a> on theories of change in evaluation.

Two pieces in the Causal Map Garden take this further:

- [Causal mapping can help reconstruct a programme theory empirically](https://garden.causalmap.app/reconstruct-theory), so the theory of change comes out of the evidence rather than a workshop.
- [Theories of change and causal maps in Ghana](https://garden.causalmap.app/summary-ghana), a book chapter with James Copestake and Fiona Remnant that sets a programme's official theory against a map coded from what people said.
